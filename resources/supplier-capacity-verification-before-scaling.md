# Supplier Capacity Verification Checklist Before You Scale a Shopify Product

This checklist helps Shopify and ecommerce operators verify whether a supplier and fulfillment workflow are ready before a product moves from testing into higher daily order volume.

It is a practical decision tool, not a guarantee of supplier performance. Confirm product-specific, destination-specific, and route-specific details before committing inventory or customer orders.

## 1. Confirm The Product And Supplier Match

- Confirm the exact product version, material, size, color, accessories, and packaging requirement.
- Ask whether the supplier is the manufacturer, a trading company, or a marketplace seller.
- Request current production capacity and the normal replenishment lead time for the exact SKU.
- Check whether stock is held, made to order, or dependent on upstream components.
- Identify at least one workable alternative supplier before scaling a volatile item.

## 2. Validate With A Controlled Test

- Order a sample or a small test batch before committing higher volume.
- Compare the approved sample with the intended production version.
- Verify product function, finish, variants, barcode or label requirements, and packaging fit.
- Record defects, missing components, or packaging issues in a repeatable QC checklist.
- Confirm how a replacement, rework, or exception would be handled.

## 3. Ask For Evidence, Not Only Promises

- Request dated product, packing, or inspection evidence for the test order where appropriate.
- Confirm what the fulfillment team checks before dispatch: product, variant, quantity, condition, and shipping label.
- Agree on which defects require a stop, rework, or seller approval.
- Clarify whether the same QC standard can be repeated as order volume increases.
- Keep the approved sample, QC notes, and packaging instructions in one handoff record.

## 4. Check Fulfillment Readiness

- Map the workflow from order import to purchase, receiving, QC, packing, dispatch, and tracking upload.
- Confirm the expected processing window for the product and current inventory condition.
- Check which shipping routes fit the target country, product characteristics, and customer delivery expectation.
- Test tracking quality and exception communication on a small set of orders.
- Confirm who owns decisions when stock, quality, address, or route problems occur.

## 5. Scale Only After The Handoff Is Stable

- Increase daily volume in steps rather than switching all orders at once.
- Monitor defect rate, processing consistency, tracking quality, and customer-impacting exceptions.
- Review supplier capacity again after a promotion, viral product event, or material change.
- Keep a fallback plan for supplier disruption or stock shortage.
