# TikTok Shop and Shopify Fulfillment Readiness Checklist for an Unexpected Demand Spike

When a product suddenly attracts far more orders than planned, the pressure usually lands on operations rather than on marketing. This checklist helps Shopify and TikTok Shop sellers work through the operational questions that tend to surface first during an unplanned demand spike.

It is a working checklist, not a performance promise. Outcomes depend on the product, supplier situation, destination market, route selection, and the seller's own operating setup.

## Verify Platform Rules Yourself

This document does not describe, interpret, or summarize TikTok Shop platform policy.

Requirements differ by market and change over time. Sellers must verify the market-specific TikTok Shop rules that apply to their own account in their own Seller Center or Policy Center, and treat that source as authoritative for anything related to handling, shipping, cancellations, or account obligations.

## How To Use This Checklist

1. Work through the sections in order; each one feeds the next.
2. Write down the answer, not the intention - an unconfirmed answer is an open risk.
3. Name one owner for every item that is unresolved.
4. Re-run the checklist when the spike changes shape: a new market, a new variant, or a new supplier.

## 1. Inventory Confirmation

Before accepting more demand, confirm what actually exists rather than what the system shows.

- [ ] Confirm physical on-hand quantity for the affected SKU and each variant.
- [ ] Identify where that stock physically sits, and whether any of it is already committed to other orders.
- [ ] Check whether the storefront inventory count reflects reality across every sales channel in use.
- [ ] Decide how oversell risk will be handled if counts and reality disagree.
- [ ] Confirm which components, inserts, or packaging materials could run out before the product itself does.
- [ ] Agree on who is allowed to change inventory settings during the spike.

## 2. Supplier Capacity

A spike tests the supplier's ability to repeat, not just to produce once.

- [ ] Confirm whether the supplier is the manufacturer, a trading company, or a reseller for this SKU.
- [ ] Ask what quantity can be produced or released, and what the replenishment lead time looks like for this exact version.
- [ ] Check whether upstream materials or components constrain the supplier before their own capacity does.
- [ ] Confirm whether the same production line, material, and specification will be used at higher volume.
- [ ] Identify at least one workable alternative source before committing further demand.
- [ ] Agree on how a shortfall will be communicated, and how early.

## 3. Quality Control

Quality control tends to degrade quietly when volume rises. Define what stays mandatory.

- [ ] Write down the checks that must not be skipped regardless of order pressure.
- [ ] Confirm product identity, variant, quantity, and included accessories against the approved reference.
- [ ] Confirm that labels, packaging version, and brand materials match the approved specification.
- [ ] Separate critical defects from cosmetic ones, and define what stops a shipment.
- [ ] Agree on the disposition path for a failed unit: hold, rework, replace, or seller review.
- [ ] Confirm that inspection evidence can still be produced and reviewed at higher volume.
- [ ] Confirm that reworked items pass the same final check as the original.

## 4. Fulfillment Handoff

The handoff between store, supplier, and warehouse is where spike orders usually stall.

- [ ] Map the path an order takes from the store to picking, QC, packing, and dispatch.
- [ ] Confirm which order data the warehouse receives, and how variant and bundle mapping is resolved.
- [ ] Confirm how address edits, duplicates, and cancellations reach the warehouse before dispatch.
- [ ] Agree on the cutoff after which an order can no longer be changed.
- [ ] Confirm how fulfillment status and tracking information return to the store record.
- [ ] Check whether the handoff still works when the queue is long, staffing is thin, or a holiday intervenes.
- [ ] Confirm what happens to orders that cannot be fulfilled from current stock.

## 5. Carrier First Scan

A dispatched label is not the same event as a parcel entering the carrier network.

- [ ] Distinguish between label creation, warehouse release, and the carrier's first scan.
- [ ] Confirm how physical carrier acceptance is evidenced for the routes in use.
- [ ] Check whether tracking numbers correspond to the correct parcels at higher batch volume.
- [ ] Confirm that tracking links resolve outside the internal system and are readable by a buyer.
- [ ] Identify parcels that have a label but no first scan, and agree who reviews that gap.
- [ ] Confirm how pickup capacity or handover scheduling changes when daily volume rises.

## 6. Exception Ownership

Under a spike, the common failure is not that problems appear but that nobody owns them.

- [ ] Name one accountable owner for stock shortages, QC failures, address changes, cancellations, and stuck parcels.
- [ ] Define how an exception is raised, where it is recorded, and what closes it.
- [ ] Agree which decisions the fulfillment side may take alone and which require seller approval.
- [ ] Confirm that substitutions are never made without an approval step.
- [ ] Define an escalation path and a backup owner for time-sensitive cases.
- [ ] Keep a record of what was decided, by whom, and when.

## 7. Buyer Communication

Buyers tolerate an operational problem more easily than silence or contradiction.

- [ ] Decide what the buyer is told at order confirmation about handling and dispatch.
- [ ] Keep the storefront message, tracking information, and support replies consistent with each other.
- [ ] Prepare wording for the exceptions most likely to occur: shortage, delay, variant change, cancellation.
- [ ] Confirm who answers buyer messages during the spike, and how quickly the queue is reviewed.
- [ ] Update the buyer when a dispatch expectation changes, rather than waiting for a complaint.
- [ ] Make sure support can see the same order and tracking evidence the buyer can see.
- [ ] Avoid statements about platform requirements in buyer messaging; keep those to the seller's own verified sources.

## Decision Checkpoints

Use these to decide whether to keep pushing demand.

**Continue** - inventory is confirmed, supplier capacity is understood, mandatory QC still runs, handoff and first scan behave normally, exceptions have owners, and buyers are receiving consistent information.

**Slow down** - one area is unconfirmed but has a named owner and a review point. Hold volume where it is until that area is answered.

**Pause intake** - inventory cannot be trusted, supplier capacity is unknown, mandatory QC is being skipped, parcels are not reaching a first scan, or exceptions have no owner.
